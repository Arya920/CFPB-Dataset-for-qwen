# Problem Statement

## AI-Assisted Complaint Intake and Smart Ticket Categorization for Banking Customer Complaints

Financial institutions receive thousands of customer complaints and service requests every day through mobile banking applications, websites, contact centers, email, and regulatory portals. These complaints are typically written in free-form natural language and often contain incomplete descriptions, ambiguous terminology, and varying levels of detail.

When submitting a complaint, customers and frontline service representatives frequently struggle to identify the correct complaint category, product type, or issue classification. As a result, complaints are often assigned to incorrect categories, requiring manual review and reassignment before reaching the appropriate operations team.

This challenge leads to several operational inefficiencies:

* Incorrect ticket categorization
* Delayed complaint resolution
* Increased manual effort for operations teams
* Multiple ticket transfers between departments
* Inconsistent classification decisions across agents
* Poor customer experience due to longer resolution times

Most existing systems rely on predefined forms, keyword-based rules, or traditional classification models that require users to manually select categories before submission. These approaches often fail to accurately interpret complex customer narratives and banking-specific terminology.

The objective of this project is to develop a domain-adapted Large Language Model (LLM) capable of automatically understanding customer complaint narratives and generating structured ticket metadata that can be directly consumed by complaint management systems.

---

# Proposed Solution

Fine-tune a banking-domain LLM using the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database to automatically convert unstructured customer complaints into structured ticket attributes.

## Input

"I reported fraudulent transactions on my debit card and the bank reversed my provisional credit without explaining the investigation outcome."

## Output

```json
{
  "product": "Checking or savings account",
  "sub_product": "Checking account",
  "issue": "Unauthorized transactions or other transaction problem",
  "sub_issue": "Debit card issue"
}
```

# Why LLM Fine-Tuning Instead of Traditional Classification?

Traditional classification models typically predict a single label and often require multiple independent models for different classification tasks.

However, complaint intake requires:

* Understanding long-form customer narratives
* Simultaneously extracting multiple business attributes
* Handling unseen complaint phrasing and terminology
* Generalizing across multiple banking products
* Producing structured outputs that integrate directly with ticketing workflows

A domain-specific LLM can learn relationships between products, issues, and customer language patterns while generating multiple structured outputs in a single inference step.
